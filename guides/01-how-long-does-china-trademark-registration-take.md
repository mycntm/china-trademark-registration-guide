# How Long Does China Trademark Registration Take? (2026 Timeline)

**Short answer: 7–9 months in practice for a clean application. The statutory ceiling is 12–15 months** — nine months for substantive examination plus a three-month opposition window, if every single stage runs to its limit. Filing itself takes one working day once you approve the quote.

Three different numbers get quoted for the same thing, and mixing them up is how launch plans go wrong: the **statutory ceiling** (what the law allows), the **practical average** (what your calendar will actually look like), and **your own file** (which depends on your mark, your classes and who else filed first). This guide separates them stage by stage, then lists what adds months and what you can do about it.

---

## The stage-by-stage timeline

| Stage | What happens | Statutory (法定) | In practice (实务) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Pre-filing search | Availability and conflict check, class strategy | — | 1–3 working days |
| 2. Drafting and filing | Goods/services wording, Chinese translation, submission to CNIPA | — | 1 day after approval |
| 3. Acceptance notice (受理通知书) | CNIPA confirms the file is complete and gives the application number | No fixed deadline | ~15 days |
| 4. Substantive examination (实质审查) | Absolute and relative grounds reviewed; citations issued | **9 months** from receipt (Art. 28) | **3–4 months** |
| 5. Publication and opposition (异议期) | Mark published in the official gazette | **3 months** (Art. 33); **2 months from 1 Jan 2027** | The full window, always |
| 6. Registration certificate | Registration recorded and certificate issued | No fixed deadline | 2–4 weeks |
| **Total** | | **12–15 months ceiling** | **7–9 months** |

The publication window is the part changing soon: China's opposition period shortens from **three months to two months for applications published on or after 1 January 2027**. If your filing lands in late 2026, expect roughly a month of schedule improvement on the back end.

## Where the time really goes

**Substantive examination is the variable.** CNIPA examines against both absolute grounds (is the mark distinctive, descriptive, deceptive?) and relative grounds (does an earlier mark or well-known brand conflict?). A smooth file clears in **three to four months** — well inside the nine-month statutory ceiling. A file that receives a refusal notice stops the clock: you have 15 days from receipt to apply for review, then the review decision itself takes six to twelve months.

**Publication is a fixed cost.** Nothing accelerates it. If nobody opposes, you simply wait out the window — three months today, two months for applications published from 1 January 2027. If someone does oppose, the case typically runs 12–18 months from opposition to decision, and registration waits behind it.

## What actually delays an application

Ordered by how often we see it:

1. **A descriptive or generic mark.** "BEST QUALITY" for clothing, or a surname-heavy brand, invites a refusal. Every refusal adds 6+ months. Distinctive invented words or a logo-plus-word filing clear far more easily.
2. **Earlier similar marks in the same class.** China has roughly 40 million live marks and adds millions a year; common English words in popular classes (9, 25, 35, 42) are crowded. A search before filing is what tells you whether to fight or refile with a different spec.
3. **Too few goods/services items, or the wrong ones.** China uses sub-classes (类似群). Ten items concentrated in one sub-class leaves you unprotected in adjacent ones — and a badly drafted spec may need correction mid-process.
4. **Slow responses to office actions.** CNIPA deadlines are short and unforgiving. Missing one is fatal to the application.
5. **Bad-faith third-party filings against you.** A squatter who files first in a neighbouring class can oppose or later cancel you.
6. **Madrid route, if you chose it.** Designating China through WIPO adds a layer of correspondence between WIPO and CNIPA. Realistic total: 12–18 months, sometimes longer, plus dependency on your base mark for the first five years.

## What you can legitimately do to go faster

- **Search first.** A proper search costs days; a refusal costs half a year. This is the single highest-leverage step.
- **File the mark you actually use, plus the Chinese transliteration.** Filing only the English word leaves the Chinese version of your brand open to a squatter.
- **Keep the spec tight and accurate.** Ten well-chosen items across the right sub-classes beat fifty sloppy ones — and in China you pay per item beyond ten anyway.
- **File class-by-class where risk varies.** If class 9 is crowded but class 42 is clear, separate filings let the clean one register while the other is argued.
- **Respond inside the deadline, always.** We track deadlines and report each stage, which is mostly about making sure nothing sits in an inbox.
- **Claim priority if you can.** A Paris Convention priority claim within six months of your home filing preserves your earlier date — useful when a competitor is circling.

## Realistic planning scenarios

| Scenario | In practice | Statutory ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| Clean mark, one class, no citations | 7–9 months | 12–15 months |
| Clean mark, 3–5 classes | 7–10 months | 12–15 months |
| One refusal notice, successful review | 15–20 months | — |
| Opposition filed against you, you win | 19–26 months | — |
| Madrid designation to China | 12–18 months | — |

Read the two columns differently. The practical column is what you plan a launch around; the statutory column is the worst case you should be prepared to survive commercially. A brand that only works if registration lands in seven months is a brand with no plan for a refusal notice.

## When to file relative to your China launch

File **before** you exhibit at a trade fair, publish a Chinese-language site, or ship product. China is first-to-file, so the filing date is what protects you — not your launch date, and not your use abroad. Companies that file after a Canton Fair booth frequently find their brand already sitting in someone else's application.
