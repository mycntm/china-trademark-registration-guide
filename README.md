## FAQ

**Can a foreign company register a trademark in China?**
Yes. Foreign companies and individuals can file directly with CNIPA. You do not need a Chinese subsidiary, a local address or a Chinese bank account — you file through a CNIPA-registered agent, which we are.

**Is China a first-to-file country?**
Yes, with narrow exceptions. Whoever files first generally wins, even if you used the brand earlier abroad. That is why filings are often made before a product launch in China, not after.

**Do I need a separate application per class?**
Yes. China follows the Nice system with 45 classes, and each class is filed and charged separately. One application can cover multiple classes, but you may want separate filings so a problem in one class does not hold up the others.

**What happens if my application is refused?**
CNIPA issues a refusal notice with reasons and a deadline to respond (typically 15 days for the notification itself, then the review period). We assess whether to argue, narrow the goods/services, or refile, and handle the review filing.

**Does a Chinese registration protect me outside China?**
No. Trademark rights are territorial. A Chinese registration covers mainland China only — Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan run separate systems and require separate filings.

**How long is a Chinese trademark valid?**
10 years from the registration date, renewable for further 10-year terms indefinitely. Renewal can be filed from 12 months before expiry, with a 6-month grace period (plus a late fee) after.
